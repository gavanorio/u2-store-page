FL700The aircraft

A glider with a jet engine.

The U-2 is a single-engine, single-seat, high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft. Its glider-like wing lets it cruise above 70,000 feet for many hours, and it is one of the most demanding aircraft in the world to land.

The Miltech Simulations U-2S brings the current US Air Force aircraft to Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024: a glass cockpit with three multi-function displays, an INS that has to be aligned before it will steer you anywhere, and custom fuel, electrical, hydraulic, oxygen, autopilot and lift spoiler systems.

It sits on a bicycle landing gear, balances on detachable pogo wheels, and on final a second pilot in a chase car talks you down to the runway. Start cold and dark, or let the EFB set the aircraft up for you.

FL680Gallery · 1955 to today

Higher than anyone could follow.

In 1954, the United States needed photographs of the Soviet Union. No fighter and no missile could reach 70,000 feet. So the answer was an aircraft that flew above all of them. Seventy years later, it still flies.

Gallery · The aircraft

Why pilots call it the hardest aircraft to fly.

Think of a very long glider with a very big jet engine. Almost everything unusual about the U-2 comes from these facts.

103 ftA glider’s wing

Much lift and very little drag. The aircraft floats, and it does not want to slow down or come down.

2 wheelsIn a line

The pogos fall away on takeoff. From then on, the pilot balances the aircraft like a bicycle.

½Of the weight is fuel

The aircraft that lands is nearly a different aircraft from the one that took off.

NoneAutomatic help

No autothrottle, no automatic spoilers, no antiskid and no differential brakes.

FL550Features

Built for the whole flight.

Cockpit

  • A glass cockpit with three multi-function displays, an up-front control display, a backup control display unit and a standby flight display.
  • Each MFD runs its own power-on test, then waits for the avionics processor. Pick a fast start or the realistic two minutes in the EFB.
  • Cold and dark really is dark: battery, generators, fuel pumps, lights, oxygen, avionics and the autopilot channels off, canopy open.
  • Battery power alone runs every display and radio. The generators or ground power keep it that way.
  • A throttle with an OFF / IDLE stop, and speedbrakes and wing lift spoilers on separate controls.

Navigation

  • The INS has to be aligned from cold and dark: 22 minutes for a full alignment, 5 for a limited one, or shorten it in the EFB.
  • In-flight alignment, GPS updating and manual position updates.
  • The steering computer flies the active destination point file, the route on the MFD NAV / DPS page and the map.
  • F/T, T/T, T/F, TTD and TFD steering, direct steer, overfly and orbits.
  • Flight director, miles and time to go, and the autopilot NAV mode all follow the destination points.
  • Enter and edit routes on the MFD, the UFCD DP page or the BCDU.

Systems

  • Five fuel tanks: two wing tanks, two auxiliary tanks and the sump that feeds the engine. Cross-transfer to balance the wings, or dump fuel.
  • Electrical power from the battery and generators, with essential and monitored buses.
  • Hydraulics, and a gear warning whenever the gear is up in the landing configuration.
  • Liquid oxygen, cabin pressure and a canopy seal you set before takeoff. Lose the cabin at altitude and hypoxia sets in.
  • Engine start with a pneumatic starting cart. In flight, the throttle AIRSTART button fires the emergency start system.
  • Autopilot with pitch, roll and yaw channels, gust control and automatic stall strips.

Sensors & EFB

  • Three sensor noses: ASARS-2, SPAN or IR/EO, plus superpods and the dorsal SPAN antenna. Change them in the EFB on the ground or in flight; the weights go into the payload.
  • Camera and radar pages drawn from live map imagery and terrain data while you are online.
  • An electronic warfare system with its own MFD page, a threat history map and a self-test.
  • The U-2 EFB app: aircraft state presets (Cold and Dark, Ready to Taxi, Ready for Takeoff), INS alignment time, avionics start-up time and ground starting air.
  • 31 normal checklists on the MFD EFA pages, from a cold and dark cockpit to shutdown.

Ground ops

  • A bicycle landing gear: one main gear, a steerable tailwheel and two pogo outriggers that hold the wings level.
  • On the takeoff roll each pogo falls away by itself once the wing carries it, between 60 and 88 knots.
  • After landing, stop and wait: the ground crew fits both pogos and levels the aircraft in about half a minute.
  • A chase car that joins you on final and calls your height in the flare.
  • Pull every gear pin and pogo flag with one click in the walkaround, or with the EFB presets.
  • Ground power and starting air on request from the EFB.

Exterior

  • Panel lines, rivet rows, stencils and antennas across the whole airframe, in physically based materials.
  • Sensor fits that change the silhouette: ASARS-2, SPAN and IR/EO noses, superpods and the dorsal antenna.
  • Navigation, beacon, landing and taxi lights.
  • The GE F118 turbofan with 17,792 lbf of static thrust.
  • 19 liveries, including the default USAF scheme.

FL420Sensors

Three sensors, live from real map data.

The U-2 exists to carry sensors. Ours has the three of the real U-2S. Fit one on the tablet, start it from the cockpit, and see its picture on the displays. With the datalink on, you can save your captures to the Datalink Portal.

Nose

ASARS-2 radar

A radar map out to 320 NM, or a close-up spot down to 0.1 NM where you can see parked aircraft and their shadows. Day, night and cloud make no difference.

Nose

SYERS-2A camera

Real satellite imagery in five bands, with zoom from 0.5x to 73x. Aim it up to 85° to the side, and it stays locked on the spot as you fly past.

Q-bay

Optical bar camera

Grainy black-and-white panoramic film, about 1,600 frames. The camera is fixed, so you fly straight lines over the target and watch the frame counter.

The sensor pictures need an internet connection.

FL380Endurance

Long days, longer nights.

2,917 US gallons in five tanks keep the U-2 up for hours. Keep the sump fed and the wings balanced, and it will still be flying at dusk.
